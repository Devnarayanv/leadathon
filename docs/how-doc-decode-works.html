Doc Decode

Understand before you sign.

Doc Decode reads everyday documents, such as rental agreements, loan papers, hospital bills, invoices, GST notices and tenders, and turns them into something a person can act on: the real amounts and dates, the clauses worth a second look, plain-language explanations in English or Malayalam, and deadlines saved to the calendar. It runs entirely on the user's own computer.

01

The problem and our solution

The problem

  • People sign rental and loan agreements they don't fully understand.
  • Legal and financial English is hard, especially for people more comfortable in Malayalam.
  • Penalties, lock-ins and deadlines are buried in long paragraphs.
  • Online AI tools need you to upload private documents to someone else's server.
  • AI summaries can invent numbers, which is dangerous with money and dates.

Our solution

  • Upload a PDF or a photo. Get the key facts, flagged clauses and deadlines.
  • Every fact shows the exact sentence and page it came from.
  • Explanations in plain English or Malayalam, with read aloud.
  • Everything runs offline on the user's machine. No cloud, no account, no database.
  • Numbers are found by rules, never by the AI, so nothing is made up.
02

The core idea: rules decide, AI explains

This is the most important design decision in the project. The work is split into two layers with very different jobs.

Layer 1 · Python rules

Decides what matters

  • Finds amounts, dates, interest rates, notice periods.
  • Detects the document type.
  • Flags risky clauses in 12 categories.
  • Checks the arithmetic on bills and invoices.
  • Same document in, same result out, every time.
Layer 2 · Local AI (Ollama)

Explains how to say it

  • Rewrites flagged clauses in simple words.
  • Answers questions using only sentences from the document.
  • Never asked to produce a number.
  • Never decides what is important.
  • Optional: if it is off, the app still works.

Why this matters: an AI model can make up a figure that looks right. By keeping every number and every decision in rules we can test, the user can trust what they see. The AI is only a presentation step on top.

06

How one document is processed

From the moment the user drops a file to the moment results appear. Purple steps are rules; the orange step uses the AI.

1

Check the upload

pipeline.py
  • File extension must be PDF, JPG or PNG.
  • Declared file type must match.
  • The first bytes of the file ("magic bytes") must really be a PDF or image, so a renamed .exe is rejected.
  • Size up to 15 MB, up to 40 pages.
2

Read the text

pdf_reader.py · ocr_reader.py
  • PyMuPDF reads the text layer of each page.
  • If a page comes back empty, it is a scan: render it at 200 DPI and run Tesseract OCR (English + Malayalam) on that page only.
  • Photos (JPG/PNG) go straight to OCR.
3

Clean and split

utils.py
  • Fix spacing and broken lines, unify Malayalam letter forms.
  • Split into clause-sized sentences, each tagged with its page number.
4

Find the document type

document_classifier.py
  • Each of the 12 types has a list of keywords in English and Malayalam.
  • Count matches, pick the highest score.
  • If the top two are too close, report "uncertain".

Example: "tenant", "landlord", "security deposit", "monthly rent" → Rental agreement

5

Extract facts, clauses and deadlines

fact_extractor.py · clause_detector.py · deadline_extractor.py · dates.py
  • Regular expressions pick out amounts (including lakh and crore), rates, dates and durations.
  • Phrase rules flag clauses in 12 categories and rank them by importance.
  • Deadline cues ("due on", "on or before", "within 30 days") are matched to dates.

Example: "a late fee of 2% per month shall be charged" → Late fee clause, page 3

6

Check, compare and build findings

verification.py · findings.py · line_items.py
  • For bills and invoices, re-add the line items and tax with exact decimals.
  • Collect every issue into one findings list with suggested actions.
7

Explain in plain words (local AI)

ai_explainer.py · prompts.py · ollama_service.py
  • Send the top 6 clauses (3 in Malayalam) to Ollama in one batched request, never the whole document.
  • The reply must be JSON; Pydantic checks it.
  • If Ollama is off, this step is skipped and rule-based notes are shown.
8

Send results to the screen

schemas.py · Analysis.jsx
  • One structured JSON response goes back to React.
  • React shows it in tabs: Overview, Extracted data, Verify, Deadlines, Findings, Tender checklist, Ask.

08

How each key part works inside

Reading scans with OCR

pdf_reader.py · ocr_reader.py
  • OCR runs only on pages that have no text, so normal PDFs stay fast.
  • Pages are rendered at 200 DPI and resized to a maximum of 2200 px, then read in English + Malayalam.
  • At most 8 pages are OCR'd, to keep a laptop responsive.

Result: a 2-page scan reads in about 5 seconds; a photo in about 1 second.

Malayalam support

utils.py · document_classifier.py · dates.py
  • Rules carry Malayalam keywords next to English ones.
  • Some Malayalam letters can be typed two ways (for example ർ and ര്). Both are converted to one form before matching.
  • Amounts like 18,500 രൂപ and durations like 11 മാസം are understood.
  • If the AI replies in the wrong script (a small model once answered in Chinese), the reply is thrown away and a warning shown.

Clause detection

clause_detector.py
Auto-renewalPenaltiesLate feesExtra chargesLock-inEarly terminationDeductionsNotice periodsInterest conditionsObligationsDeadlinesOne-sided conditions
  • Each category has a set of trigger phrases. A matching sentence is flagged with its page.
  • Flags are "worth a look", never "illegal". A test makes sure no rule uses words like illegal or void.

AI explanations

ai_explainer.py · prompts.py
  • All selected clauses go in one request, not one per clause, to save time.
  • Document text is placed inside a fenced block; the AI is told to ignore any instructions inside it. This blocks "prompt injection" hidden in a document.
  • If the reply is cut off, every complete item is still kept instead of retrying the whole thing.
  • The model is loaded when the server starts and kept warm for 30 minutes.

Ask the document

question_answering.py
  • A small hand-written TF-IDF search scores every sentence against the question.
  • Only the top sentences go to the AI, so long documents cost the same as short ones.
  • The answer is shown together with those sentences as evidence.

Why no vector database: it adds weight and setup for little gain on single documents.

Verifying the maths

verification.py · money.py
  • Every amount goes through Python's Decimal, never floating point, so ₹1,23,456.78 adds up exactly.
  • Each check shows the formula, the inputs, what the document says and what the maths gives.
  • A missing value is reported as missing, never treated as zero.

Deadlines and calendar

deadline_extractor.py · calendar_export.py
  • Finds phrases like "due on", "on or before", "within 15 days" and links them to a date.
  • Dates that can't be confirmed are kept apart and never shown as overdue.
  • Ticked dates are exported as a standard .ics file (RFC 5545) that any calendar opens.

Read aloud

AudioControls.jsx · tts_service.py
  • Uses the browser's own voice when it has one for the language.
  • Windows has no Malayalam voice, so the backend uses eSpeak NG offline.
  • If neither is available the button is disabled with a reason, instead of reading Malayalam in an English voice.

Privacy and security

What we do

  • The file stays in memory for one request, then is dropped. Never written to disk.
  • No database, no history. Reload the page and it's gone.
  • No cloud AI, no API key. The AI runs at localhost.
  • Uploads checked by extension, file type and magic bytes. Nothing uploaded is ever run.
  • Document text is never written to logs.
  • Document text is fenced in prompts to block prompt injection. A test covers this.

What we don't claim

  • It's a hackathon prototype, not a hardened product.
  • No login, no encryption at rest, no rate limiting.
  • Meant to run on your own machine, not on a public network.
  • The browser may use an online voice for read aloud, depending on the system.

Limitations and future work

Current limitations

  • Rules match wording, so an unusually phrased clause can be missed.
  • Handwriting can't be read reliably; Tesseract is for printed text.
  • Blurry photos give poor OCR, and poor text gives poor facts.
  • Malayalam explanations need a larger, slower AI model.
  • Not legal or financial advice.

Future work

  • Show rule results instantly, then stream AI explanations in.
  • Mobile app that photographs and reads documents on the phone.
  • More Indian languages: Tamil, Hindi, Kannada.
  • Handwriting recognition model.
  • Printable one-page summary with key terms and dates.

14

Questions we expect

How is this different from ChatGPT or an AI PDF summariser?

Those send your document to the cloud and let the AI decide everything, including numbers. Doc Decode runs offline, and every fact and flag comes from testable rules. The AI only rewrites in simpler words.

What if the AI gives a wrong explanation?

The original clause is always shown beside it, with its page, and each explanation is labelled as coming from the AI or the rules. The AI is never trusted with numbers.

Does it need the internet?

No. Once installed, everything runs on the laptop: the browser app, the Python backend and the AI model.

Why Ollama and not the OpenAI or Gemini API?

Privacy and cost. Personal documents never leave the machine, and there is no API key or per-use charge.

Why use rules at all? Isn't AI better?

Rules are predictable and testable: the same document always gives the same result, and we have 143 tests to prove it. For money and dates, being reliably right matters more than being clever.

What happens with a document type you don't support?

It is marked "uncertain" or "other" and still gets the general amount, date and clause rules. The user can also pick the type manually.

How do you handle Malayalam?

At three levels: Tesseract reads Malayalam scans, the rules carry Malayalam keywords, and a larger model writes Malayalam explanations. If the AI answers in the wrong script, we discard it.

How do you stop a malicious document from tricking the AI?

Document text is placed in a fenced block and the AI is told to treat it as data, not instructions. There is a test for this.

How did you make it fast on a 4 GB laptop GPU?

A small 3B model that fits fully in GPU memory, one batched request for all clauses, preloading the model at startup, and OCR only on pages that need it.

Who would use this?

Tenants, borrowers, patients' families, small business owners handling invoices, GST notices and tenders, especially people more comfortable in Malayalam than legal English.
